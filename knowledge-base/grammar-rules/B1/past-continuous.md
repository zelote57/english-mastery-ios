# B1_INIT: Past continuous

Form the past continuous with **was/were + verb-ing**: “I was reading” and “they were sleeping.” Use it to describe an action in progress at a particular past time or to provide background for another event. The past simple often describes the shorter event: “I was reading when the phone rang.” **While** commonly introduces an action in progress, although it can also express contrast in other contexts.

The past continuous does not automatically mean that the action was completed. Spanish may use the imperfect or **estar + gerundio** for a similar background action, but the English auxiliary **was/were** is required.

- ❌ “She reading when I called.” → ✅ “She was reading when I called.” — Ella estaba leyendo cuando llamé.
- ❌ “They was cooking.” → ✅ “They were cooking.” — Ellos estaban cocinando.
- ❌ “I was read at eight.” → ✅ “I was reading at eight.” — Estaba leyendo a las ocho.
- ❌ “While he was sleeping, the phone was rang.” → ✅ “While he was sleeping, the phone rang.” — Mientras dormía, sonó el teléfono.

Choose **was** for I/he/she/it and **were** for you/we/they. Some state verbs are not normally used in the continuous for their ordinary state meaning.
