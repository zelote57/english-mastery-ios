# B2_ADV: Spanish interference and false friends

False friends are words that resemble one another across languages but do not always share the intended meaning. Check the context rather than assuming that a familiar spelling guarantees a translation. **Actually** usually means “in fact”, while Spanish **actualmente** is often “currently” or “at present”. **Realize** commonly means “become aware”; Spanish **realizar** can mean “carry out” or “perform”.

**Sensible** usually means reasonable or practical; Spanish **sensible** is often “sensitive”. **Embarrassed** means feeling awkward or ashamed, whereas **embarazada** means “pregnant”. A word may have additional senses in other contexts, so the intended sentence matters.

- ❌ “Actually I live in Bogotá,” when the meaning is *actualmente* → ✅ “Currently I live in Bogotá.” — Actualmente vivo en Bogotá.
- ❌ “We realized the project,” when the meaning is *realizamos el proyecto* → ✅ “We carried out the project.” — Realizamos el proyecto.
- ❌ “He is very sensible to noise,” when the meaning is *sensible al ruido* → ✅ “He is very sensitive to noise.” — Es muy sensible al ruido.
- ❌ “She is embarrassed,” when the meaning is *está embarazada* → ✅ “She is pregnant.” — Está embarazada.

The original English sentences may be grammatical with different meanings. The error is a mismatch with the intended Spanish meaning, not necessarily incorrect grammar.
