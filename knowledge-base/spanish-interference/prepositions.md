# A2_ADV: Spanish interference and prepositions

English prepositions do not map one-to-one to Spanish prepositions. Learn common combinations with their verbs and meanings. **Depend on** expresses dependence: “It depends on the weather.” **Dream about** or **dream of** can refer to a dream or an imagined possibility, although their precise meanings depend on context.

Use **marry someone** without a preposition before the person, or **be/get married to someone**. The Spanish expression **casarse con** does not imply **marry with** in this meaning. English does use “marry with” in other meanings, such as combining two ideas, so avoid treating every occurrence as universally impossible.

- ❌ “It depends of the weather.” → ✅ “It depends on the weather.” — Depende del tiempo.
- ❌ “I dream with travelling,” meaning *sueño con viajar* → ✅ “I dream of travelling.” — Sueño con viajar.
- ❌ “She married with Luis,” meaning *se casó con Luis* → ✅ “She married Luis.” — Ella se casó con Luis.
- ❌ “He is married with Ana,” meaning *está casado con Ana* → ✅ “He is married to Ana.” — Está casado con Ana.

For **in, on**, and **at** with place and time, consider the complete expression and its meaning; “in the morning”, “on Monday”, and “at noon” are common patterns.
