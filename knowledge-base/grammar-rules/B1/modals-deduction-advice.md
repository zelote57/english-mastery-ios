# B1_ADV: Modals of deduction and advice

Use **must + base verb** for a strong logical conclusion: “The lights are off; they must be asleep.” Use **might/may/could + base verb** for a possibility: “They might be asleep.” Use **can't + base verb** for a strong conclusion that something is impossible: “That can't be the answer.” These are deductions, not guarantees; their force depends on the evidence.

Use **should + base verb** to give advice: “You should rest.” The modal is followed by the base form without **to** or third-person **-s**. **Must** can also express obligation in another context, so distinguish obligation from deduction by meaning.

- ❌ “She must to be tired.” → ✅ “She must be tired.” — Debe de estar cansada.
- ❌ “He might knows.” → ✅ “He might know.” — Quizá lo sepa.
- ❌ “You should to rest.” → ✅ “You should rest.” — Deberías descansar.
- ❌ “It can't is true.” → ✅ “It can't be true.” — No puede ser verdad.

Spanish **debe** can suggest either obligation or inference. Explain the interpretation from the evidence in the sentence instead of assuming one meaning.
