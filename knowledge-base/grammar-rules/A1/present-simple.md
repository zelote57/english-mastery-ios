# A1: Simple present

Use the simple present for habits, routines and general facts: “I work on Mondays.” In affirmative statements, add **-s** or the appropriate **-es** form to the verb with **he, she**, or **it**: “She works” and “He watches”. A consonant plus **y** generally changes to **-ies**, as in “she studies”.

Use **do** with I/you/we/they and **does** with he/she/it for most questions and negatives. After **do, does, don't**, or **doesn't**, use the base verb: “Does she work?” and “She doesn't work.” The verb **be** forms questions and negatives differently: “Is she ready?”

- ❌ “She work every day.” → ✅ “She works every day.” — Ella trabaja todos los días.
- ❌ “Does he works here?” → ✅ “Does he work here?” — ¿Trabaja él aquí?
- ❌ “He don't study.” → ✅ “He doesn't study.” — Él no estudia.
- ❌ “I am work on Sundays.” → ✅ “I work on Sundays.” — Trabajo los domingos.

Spanish verb endings often identify the subject; English usually needs a separate subject pronoun.
