# A1: Verb to be

The English verb **to be** can express identity, occupation, origin, age, feelings and location. In the present tense, choose the form that agrees with the subject: **I am**, **you are**, **he is**, **she is**, **it is**, **we are**, and **they are**. A singular person or thing usually takes **is**; a plural subject takes **are**. Even when Spanish leaves the subject implicit, an ordinary English sentence generally needs an explicit subject.

In a statement, put the subject before the verb: “I am a student.” In a question, put the verb before the subject: “Are you a student?” To make a negative, place **not** after the verb: “She is not a teacher.” Common contractions include **I'm**, **you're**, **he's**, **we're**, **isn't**, and **aren't**. The short answer to “Are they ready?” is “Yes, they are” or “No, they aren't.”

For Spanish speakers, one common difficulty is that Spanish uses both **ser** and **estar** where English often uses **to be**. English also uses **be**, not **have**, when stating age: “I am twenty years old.” Do not choose an English verb by translating each Spanish word separately; identify the subject and the meaning of the whole sentence.

Compare these examples:

- ❌ “She are a doctor.” → ✅ “She is a doctor.” — Ella es médica.
- ❌ “They is at home.” → ✅ “They are at home.” — Ellos están en casa.
- ❌ “I have twenty years.” → ✅ “I am twenty years old.” — Tengo veinte años.
- ❌ “Is raining.” → ✅ “It is raining.” — Está lloviendo.

When checking a response, first find the subject, then choose **am**, **is**, or **are**. Check word order separately for statements and questions. Contractions are valid when they represent the same subject and form of the verb.
