# A1: Personal pronouns

Personal subject pronouns identify who or what performs an action. Use **I** for yourself, **you** for the person addressed, **he** or **she** for a person, **it** for a thing or animal when its sex is not relevant, **we** for a group including the speaker, and **they** for other people or things. English subject pronouns are normally explicit, unlike Spanish subject pronouns that can often be omitted.

Use an object pronoun after a verb or preposition: **me, you, him, her, it, us, them**. Choose the form by its role in the English sentence, not by whether the Spanish translation contains a separate pronoun.

- ❌ “Is my friend.” → ✅ “She is my friend.” — Ella es mi amiga.
- ❌ “Me work here.” → ✅ “I work here.” — Yo trabajo aquí.
- ❌ “I see they.” → ✅ “I see them.” — Los veo.
- ❌ “My book is new. He is blue.” → ✅ “My book is new. It is blue.” — Mi libro es nuevo. Es azul.

For people, follow the person's pronouns when known; **they** can also refer to one person. In an ordinary English statement, check that the subject appears before the verb.
