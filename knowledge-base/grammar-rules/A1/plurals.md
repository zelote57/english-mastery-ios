# A1: Regular plurals

Most regular English countable nouns form the plural by adding **-s**: “one book, two books”. Add **-es** after common endings such as **-s, -ss, -sh, -ch**, and **-x**: “one box, two boxes”. For a consonant followed by **y**, change **y** to **i** and add **-es**: “city, cities”. After a vowel plus **y**, usually add only **-s**: “boy, boys”.

The plural noun normally follows a number greater than one. Spanish also marks nouns as plural, but the English spelling rules differ; do not assume that adding just **s** works for every ending. Irregular plurals such as “children” are separate forms and do not follow these regular patterns.

- ❌ “three book” → ✅ “three books” — tres libros.
- ❌ “two boxs” → ✅ “two boxes” — dos cajas.
- ❌ “many citys” → ✅ “many cities” — muchas ciudades.
- ❌ “two boies” → ✅ “two boys” — dos niños.

Check the last letters of the singular noun before choosing the plural ending.
