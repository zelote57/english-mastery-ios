# A2_INT: Comparatives and superlatives

Use a comparative to compare two people or things: a short adjective often takes **-er + than** (“taller than”); many longer adjectives use **more + adjective + than** (“more interesting than”). For a superlative, use **the** with **-est** or **most**: “the tallest” and “the most interesting”. **Good** and **bad** have irregular forms: **better/best** and **worse/worst**.

Spelling can change: “big, bigger, biggest” doubles the final consonant, and “happy, happier, happiest” changes **y** to **i**. Do not combine **more** with an **-er** comparative. Spanish commonly uses **más** before the adjective, but English often changes the adjective itself.

- ❌ “A train is more fast than a bus.” → ✅ “A train is faster than a bus.” — Un tren es más rápido que un autobús.
- ❌ “This book is interestinger.” → ✅ “This book is more interesting.” — Este libro es más interesante.
- ❌ “She is the more tall.” → ✅ “She is the tallest.” — Ella es la más alta.
- ❌ “Today is gooder.” → ✅ “Today is better.” — Hoy es mejor.

“Bigger” and “larger” can both be suitable depending on meaning and context.
