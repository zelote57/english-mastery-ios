# B1_INT: Gerunds vs infinitives

Some English verbs are followed by a gerund (**verb-ing**): “enjoy reading” and “avoid driving.” Others are followed by **to + base verb**: “decide to leave” and “want to learn.” Learn the pattern with each verb; it cannot always be predicted from its Spanish equivalent.

After a preposition, a following verb generally takes the **-ing** form: “interested in learning” and “look forward to seeing you.” In the latter expression, **to** is a preposition, not the infinitive marker. Some verbs accept both patterns with a difference in meaning, for example “stop smoking” versus “stop to smoke”.

- ❌ “I enjoy to read.” → ✅ “I enjoy reading.” — Disfruto de la lectura.
- ❌ “She decided leaving.” → ✅ “She decided to leave.” — Ella decidió irse.
- ❌ “They are interested in learn English.” → ✅ “They are interested in learning English.” — Les interesa aprender inglés.
- ❌ “I look forward to see you.” → ✅ “I look forward to seeing you.” — Espero verte con ilusión.

Use the surrounding verb or preposition to choose the form, and check meaning when more than one construction is possible.
