# B1_ADV: Core phrasal verbs

A phrasal verb combines a verb and one or more particles, often with a meaning that is not obvious from the separate words. **Give up** can mean stop trying; **turn on** can mean activate a device; **look forward to** means anticipate something with pleasure. Learn each expression in a complete example.

Some transitive phrasal verbs are separable: “turn on the light” and “turn the light on” are both valid. With a pronoun object, place it between verb and particle: “turn it on”, not “turn on it”. **Look forward to** is not separable, and its **to** is a preposition followed by a noun or **-ing**.

- ❌ “Turn on it.” → ✅ “Turn it on.” — Enciéndelo.
- ❌ “She gave the project up it.” → ✅ “She gave it up.” — Ella lo abandonó.
- ❌ “I look forward to meet you.” → ✅ “I look forward to meeting you.” — Espero conocerte con ilusión.
- ❌ “He gave up to smoke.” → ✅ “He gave up smoking.” — Dejó de fumar.

Spanish often expresses the same idea with a single verb. Do not translate each English particle separately; check the pattern of the specific phrasal verb.
