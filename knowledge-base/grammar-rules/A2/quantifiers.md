# A2_ADV: Quantifiers

Use **many** with plural countable nouns (“many books”) and **much** with uncountable nouns (“much water”), especially in questions and negatives. **A lot of** works with both: “a lot of books” and “a lot of water.” Use **some** in many affirmative statements and **any** in many questions and negatives.

These are useful starting patterns, not absolute bans: **some** is natural in offers and requests (“Would you like some tea?”), and **any** can mean “it does not matter which” in an affirmative sentence. English countability may differ from Spanish; for example, **information** is normally uncountable in English.

- ❌ “How much books do you have?” → ✅ “How many books do you have?” — ¿Cuántos libros tienes?
- ❌ “There aren't many water.” → ✅ “There isn't much water.” — No hay mucha agua.
- ❌ “I need an information.” → ✅ “I need some information.” — Necesito información.
- ❌ “There isn't any apples.” → ✅ “There aren't any apples.” — No hay manzanas.

First identify whether the noun is countable and whether it is singular or plural; then consider the meaning of the whole sentence.
