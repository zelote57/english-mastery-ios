# A2_ADV: Prepositions of place and time

English often uses **in**, **on**, and **at** to describe place or time, but the choice depends on the expression rather than on a word-for-word translation from Spanish. Treat common combinations as patterns and pay attention to the noun that follows the preposition.

For time, use **at** with a clock time: “at seven o'clock.” Use **on** with days and dates: “on Monday” and “on 5 May.” Use **in** with months, years, seasons, and longer periods: “in July,” “in 2025,” and “in the morning.” Some fixed expressions differ from the general pattern, such as **at night** and **on Monday morning**.

For place, **in** commonly indicates an enclosed area or a city or country: “in the room” and “in Madrid.” **On** commonly indicates a surface: “on the table.” **At** often identifies a point or meeting place: “at the station.” The same place can take different prepositions depending on the meaning: “at the school” identifies a location, while “in the school” emphasizes being inside the building.

Spanish **en** can correspond to several English prepositions, so translating it automatically as **in** is unreliable. Check whether the expression describes a clock time, a day, a longer period, a surface, an enclosed space, or a point. Learn fixed expressions together with their prepositions.

Compare:

- ❌ “The lesson starts in nine.” → ✅ “The lesson starts at nine.” — La clase empieza a las nueve.
- ❌ “We meet in Monday.” → ✅ “We meet on Monday.” — Nos reunimos el lunes.
- ❌ “The book is in the table.” → ✅ “The book is on the table.” — El libro está sobre la mesa.
- ❌ “I study at the morning.” → ✅ “I study in the morning.” — Estudio por la mañana.

Other verbs have their own prepositions, such as **depend on**. These combinations need to be learned as units rather than inferred only from the Spanish preposition.
