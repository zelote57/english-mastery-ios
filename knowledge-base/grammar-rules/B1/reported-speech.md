# B1_ADV: Reported speech

Reported speech conveys what someone said without necessarily repeating the exact words. With a past reporting verb, the verb in the reported clause often moves back in time: present simple to past simple, or **will** to **would**. “She said, ‘I am tired’” can become “She said that she was tired.” Change pronouns and time expressions when the new speaker's perspective requires it.

Use **say** without a direct person object (“She said that…”), or **tell + person** (“She told me that…”). **That** is often optional in statements. Backshift is common, not mandatory in every context: a statement still true now can sometimes retain its original tense.

- ❌ “She said me she was tired.” → ✅ “She told me she was tired.” — Me dijo que estaba cansada.
- ❌ “He told that he was ready.” → ✅ “He said that he was ready.” — Dijo que estaba listo.
- ❌ “Ana said I am tired,” when reporting Ana's past words → ✅ “Ana said she was tired.” — Ana dijo que estaba cansada.

Spanish **decir** covers both English patterns; check whether the sentence names a person after the reporting verb. Keep the original meaning and adjust viewpoint carefully.
