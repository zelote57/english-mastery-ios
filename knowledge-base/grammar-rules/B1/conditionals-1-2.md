# B1_INT: First and second conditionals

The first conditional often describes a possible future situation and its result: **if + present simple, will + base verb**. For example, “If it rains, we will stay home.” The if-clause normally does not use **will** to express the future condition itself. Other modals and imperatives are also possible when the meaning calls for them.

The second conditional commonly describes an unreal or hypothetical present or future situation: **if + past simple, would + base verb**. “If I had more time, I would learn Italian” suggests that I do not have that time now. **If I were** is a standard form for hypothetical situations; **if I was** is also used in informal English.

- ❌ “If it will rain, we will stay home.” → ✅ “If it rains, we will stay home.” — Si llueve, nos quedaremos en casa.
- ❌ “If I have more time, I would travel.” → ✅ “If I had more time, I would travel.” — Si tuviera más tiempo, viajaría.
- ❌ “If she knew, she would tells us.” → ✅ “If she knew, she would tell us.” — Si lo supiera, nos lo diría.

Spanish conditional forms do not map word-for-word to these English patterns. Decide whether the situation is realistically possible or hypothetical before selecting the form.
