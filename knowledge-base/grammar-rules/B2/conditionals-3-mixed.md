# B2_INIT: Third and mixed conditionals

The third conditional commonly describes an unreal past condition and its unreal past result: **if + past perfect, would have + past participle**. “If I had studied, I would have passed” presents both studying and passing as contrary to the past facts. Other modals such as **could have** or **might have** can express ability or possibility instead of a certain result.

A mixed conditional can connect different times. **If + past perfect, would + base verb** can describe an unreal past cause and a present result: “If I had taken that job, I would live in Paris now.” Conversely, “If I were more careful, I wouldn't have made that mistake” links a hypothetical present characteristic to a past result.

- ❌ “If I would have studied, I would have passed.” → ✅ “If I had studied, I would have passed.” — Si hubiera estudiado, habría aprobado.
- ❌ “If she had left earlier, she would arrive on time yesterday.” → ✅ “If she had left earlier, she would have arrived on time yesterday.” — Si hubiera salido antes, habría llegado a tiempo ayer.
- ❌ “If I had accepted the job, I would have live there now.” → ✅ “If I had accepted the job, I would live there now.” — Si hubiera aceptado el trabajo, viviría allí ahora.
