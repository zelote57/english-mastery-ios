# A2_INT: Future with "going to" and "will"

Use **be going to + base verb** for an existing intention or a prediction based on present evidence: “She is going to study” and “Look at those clouds; it is going to rain.” The form of **be** agrees with the subject. In questions, put **be** before the subject: “Are they going to leave?”

Use **will + base verb** for a decision made as you speak, a promise, or a prediction: “I'll help you” and “It will be cold tomorrow.” Use **won't** for the negative. Both forms can express predictions, so context matters; they are not interchangeable in every exercise, but some sentences permit either.

- ❌ “She going to travel.” → ✅ “She is going to travel.” — Ella va a viajar.
- ❌ “Are you going travel?” → ✅ “Are you going to travel?” — ¿Vas a viajar?
- ❌ “He will calls.” → ✅ “He will call.” — Él llamará.
- ❌ “I won't to forget.” → ✅ “I won't forget.” — No lo olvidaré.

Spanish uses several future expressions too. Avoid adding **to** after **will**, even when a Spanish infinitive follows a future expression.
