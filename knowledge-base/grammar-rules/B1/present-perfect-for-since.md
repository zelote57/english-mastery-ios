# B1_INIT: Present perfect and for vs since

Form the present perfect with **have/has + past participle**: “I have visited” and “she has seen”. Use it for experiences without a specified finished past time, and for situations that began in the past and continue now. A finished time such as **yesterday** normally calls for the past simple: “I saw her yesterday.”

In questions, **ever** can mean “at any time”; **never** means “at no time.” **Already** often indicates something has happened earlier than expected, and **yet** often appears in questions or negative sentences about something expected. Several adverbs can be grammatical in a question but convey different meanings, so choose according to the intended meaning.

Use **for** with a duration (“for three years”) and **since** with a starting point (“since 2021”). Spanish often uses the present tense with **desde hace** for an ongoing situation; English commonly uses the present perfect.

- ❌ “She has went home.” → ✅ “She has gone home.” — Ella se ha ido a casa.
- ❌ “I have seen him yesterday.” → ✅ “I saw him yesterday.” — Lo vi ayer.
- ❌ “I have lived here since three years.” → ✅ “I have lived here for three years.” — Vivo aquí desde hace tres años.
- ❌ “We have worked here for 2021.” → ✅ “We have worked here since 2021.” — Trabajamos aquí desde 2021.
